The Capability Ghost

The trick

The marker's key was hiding in plain metadata, the TAG in a PAX user xattr and the EPOCH in a journal field, and the cipher was spelled out in the binary's own --help, so the whole thing unseals statically without root.

01Start point: unzip the handout and read the records as data.

  • What I found: capability-ghost.zip holds runner-fs.tar.gz, runner.journal and a manifest. The tar carries PAX extended attributes. /root/.bash_history is the whole story in miniature: setcap cap_dac_read_search+ep /usr/local/bin/hc-probe TAG=$(head -c8 /dev/urandom | xxd -p) setfattr -n user.build_seal -v "0x$TAG" /usr/local/bin/hc-probe EPOCH=$(date +%s) /usr/local/bin/hc-probe --seal "$TAG" "$EPOCH" /etc/solunet/signaling-cfg /etc/solunet/signaling-cfg.sealed shred -u /etc/solunet/signaling-cfg
  • Thinking: the plaintext config was shredded, so the marker only survives inside signaling-cfg.sealed (27 bytes). To unseal it I need the TAG, the EPOCH, and hc-probe's sealing algorithm. The "capability" theme (CAP_DAC_READ_ SEARCH on hc-probe) is a decoy about how the file was read; the real key material is the TAG xattr and the EPOCH.

02Pivot: read the stored xattrs straight from the tar's PAX headers (no extraction, no root).

  • What I found: on /usr/local/bin/hc-probe, PAX record SCHILY.xattr.user.build_seal = 8 raw bytes 509df6cd8e103fa5. That is the TAG (the history wrote it as "0x$TAG"). The security.capability xattrs are present but are not key material.

03Pivot: get the EPOCH from the journal.

  • What I found: scanning runner.journal (a real systemd journal; readable as data) the hc-probe event carries a custom field X_SEAL_EPOCH=1793294042, with MESSAGE "health probe sealed result written". The brief warns this is the seal epoch, not the entry's own timestamp, so I take X_SEAL_EPOCH exactly.

04Pivot: recover the sealing algorithm from hc-probe itself (static only).

  • What I did: carved hc-probe out of the tar and read its strings. The binary documents its own cipher in its help text: "key = HMAC-SHA256(key = raw_bytes(tag_hex), msg = ascii_decimal_string(seal_epoch))" and "out[i] = in[i] XOR key[i mod 32]" (and notes --seal and --unseal are the same XOR operation). So I never run it.
  • Result: key = HMAC-SHA256(key = bytes.fromhex(TAG), msg = b"1793294042"); XOR that 32-byte keystream, repeating, over the 27 sealed bytes. Plaintext is the marker.

Defensive takeaways

Extended attributes and journal custom fields travel with archives and images, so treat them as evidence and, defensively, as a place secrets leak: a shredded plaintext was still recoverable because its key lived in xattrs and logs. Deriving a seal key from a short tag plus a timestamp, both recorded alongside the ciphertext, is not protection. Keys belong in a KMS or HSM, not in file metadata and logs next to the data they protect.
